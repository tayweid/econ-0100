## Q1 | The Witching Hour’s Workshop

The Witching Hour makes its novelty cauldrons by hand. The table shows how many cauldrons the workshop makes in a day with each number of workers. Each worker is paid 60 coins a day, and the workshop pays 120 coins a day in rent however many cauldrons it makes.

::: {.table style=grid inset=7pt columns="auto auto 1fr 1fr 1fr 1fr" fills=r0:gray-dark valign=middle}

| **Workers** | **Cauldrons** | **Marginal product** | **Variable cost** | **Total cost** | **ATC** |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 0 | 0 | — | [0]{.red} | [120]{.red} | — |
| 1 | 15 | [15]{.red} | [60]{.red} | [180]{.red} | [12]{.red} |
| 2 | 24 | [9]{.red} | [120]{.red} | [240]{.red} | [10]{.red} |
| 3 | 30 | [6]{.red} | [180]{.red} | [300]{.red} | [10]{.red} |
| 4 | 32 | [2]{.red} | [240]{.red} | [360]{.red} | [11.25]{.red} |

:::

a\) Fill in the marginal product of each worker. What happens to the marginal product as workers are added?

::: solution

The marginal products are 15, 9, 6 and 2. Each added worker adds fewer cauldrons than the one before: the marginal product is diminishing.

:::

b\) Fill in the variable cost, total cost, and average total cost at each number of workers.

::: solution

Variable cost is the wage bill, $60 \times L$. Total cost adds the rent of 120. Average total cost is total cost divided by cauldrons: $180/15 = 12$, $240/24 = 10$, $300/30 = 10$, $360/32 = 11.25$. It falls, flattens, then rises.

:::

c\) The first worker costs 60 coins and adds 15 cauldrons. The fourth worker costs 60 coins and adds 2 cauldrons. What does each cauldron add to total cost in each case? Use your answer to explain why The Witching Hour has increasing marginal costs.

::: solution

The first worker’s cauldrons each add $60/15 = 4$ coins to total cost. The fourth worker’s cauldrons each add $60/2 = 30$ coins. Every worker costs the same, but later workers add fewer cauldrons, so each extra cauldron costs more. Diminishing marginal product is the reason marginal cost rises. (The third worker’s cauldrons each add $60/6 = 10$, the same as average total cost at 30 cauldrons: marginal cost meets $ATC$ at its lowest point.)

:::
