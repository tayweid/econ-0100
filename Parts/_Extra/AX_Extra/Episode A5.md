### The Map

Today is the first day of the rest of the class. 

Last week we began the journey into microeconomic theory where economics all began. Scarcity constrains our ability to meet all our wants which requires us to make choices. We found the definition of the outer edge of our constraints, the PPF, and that choices along the frontier require tradeoffs. Then when we formally defined the cost of each tradeoff as opportunity cost we realized we could minimize what we gave up by specializing and trading, effectively increasing what is attainable. This is the magic that accompanies clear economic thinking.

During our discussion of these foundational limitations at the heart of economics, we conveniently avoided saying anything too emphatic about WHERE we would like to be on the frontier. All we said is that the frontier is where we would like to be, and that opportunity cost is at the heart of how we conceive of tradeoffs. With all this in mind, we’re ready to begin thinking about how we think about where on the frontier we would like to be. 

This is a highly non-linear map of the outlines of microeconomics.
