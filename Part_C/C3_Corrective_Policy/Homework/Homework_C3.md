## Q1 | Malodorous Byproducts

Butterbeer is beloved by Hogwarts students and teachers alike and is widely available at wizarding shops. But the beverage has the negative effect of generating displeasing odors from its imbibers. Preferences for butterbeer and its marginal cost can be represented by the following relationships.

$$
D: P_B = 40 - Q_D \qquad\qquad S: P_S = 10 + \frac{1}{2} Q_S
$$

Prices are in galleons and quantity is in bottles. The odor imposes a cost of 9 galleons per bottle on everyone nearby.

Using your economic policy advising credentials, offer a remedy to the malodorous byproduct the Ministry could implement. And conclude with a description of the effects your policy would have on the butterbeer market.

## Q2 | Dragon Pox

Dragon Pox is spreading through the wizarding world. A dose of Gorsemoor’s Draught keeps a witch or wizard from catching it, so whoever drinks it also can’t pass Dragon Pox on to anyone else. The marginal private benefit of a dose is $P = 24 - Q$, and supply, which is both the private and the social cost, is $P = 4 + Q$. Prices are in galleons and quantity is in doses. Each dose also gives a benefit of 6 galleons to everyone else, since they are less likely to catch Dragon Pox.

Offer a remedy the Ministry could implement to correct the market failure. And conclude with a description of the effects your policy would have on the market for Gorsemoor’s Draught.

 

 

 

 

 

Policy (tax or subsidy): \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Size per dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

Market Quantity: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ Quantity with Your Policy: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

How does your policy bring the market to the socially efficient quantity?

> a\) It lowers the cost of brewing each dose, so sellers offer fewer.
>
> b\) It raises the price buyers pay, so fewer witches and wizards drink it.
>
> c\) Buyers now receive the benefit to everyone else on every dose, so their benefit plus the subsidy matches marginal social benefit, and the market settles where marginal social benefit equals marginal cost.
>
> d\) It sets a price floor, so sellers earn more on every dose.
