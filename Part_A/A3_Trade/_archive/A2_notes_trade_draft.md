So far we’ve looked at a type of cooperation between two individuals. Here the setup here is a little different, where instead of forming a guild together, they both aim to maximize their own harvest individually. This is where the real magic happens. So to model this environment, we'll set up two PPFs one for Molly and one for Andrew.

In autarky the farmers don't trade with each other and, like before, these PPFs represent the frontier for each farm. In autarky, then, Molly can grow $5$ tons of carrots OR $45$ tons of spinach while Andrew can grow $4$ tons of carrots OR $18$ tons of spinach. Lets pick a point in autarky and see if we can do better with trade, say $1$ ton of carrots and $36$ tons of spinach for Molly, and $3$ tons of carrots and $7$ tons of spinach for Andrew.

So what if we realized that since we could exceed the joint PPF from last time, we wondered if we could make both farms better off simultaneously. We have a hunch that since minimizing opportunity cost by specializing according to comparative advantage improves the whole, that we might be able to follow a similar program and improve both farmers. But additionally, to give both farmers access to both crops, we also need to trade.

The first thing to realize when we're thinking about trading is that each farmer can perform a sort of trade with themself. Each farmer could, if they wanted to, switch part of their farm from the crop they're growing under specialization to the other good. Thought of as a trade, this exchange is exactly equal to their opportunity cost, and is what the PPF tells us. So we're looking for any trade that is more favorable than their PPF. We can represent this with a line. As long as the trade lives outside the farmer's PPF, the trade benefits them.

To visualize this trade we just need to realize that when we give up the good that we're specializing in, we also receive the other good in exchange. This lets us view the trade as a downward sloping relationship between the two goods, where the slope represents the exchange rate. And this is going to look very similar to a PPF except it's not going to intercept our two extremes. It's just going to run through the point where we're currently producing.
