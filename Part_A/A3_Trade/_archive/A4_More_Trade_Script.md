When thinking about trades, we have two levers. First, the exchange rate, represented by the slope of the trade line. If the trade is too steep, its great for one farmer and terrible the other. And if its too flat, the same but reversed. So the only trades that are acceptible happen when the exchange rate lies between the opportunity costs of the two farmers.

And second, we can choose how much of each goods the farmers grow. Earlier we showed that Molly has a comparative advantage and spinach and Andrew has a comparative advantage in carrots so each is gonna specialize in each of these two goods. Each farmer specializing according to their comparative advantage, Molly harvests $45$ tons of spinach and Andrew harvests $4$ units of carrots. This is like the guild solution, but here we're looking for a trade that makes both farmers better off. And it's important to note here that we STILL haven't imposed preferences. All we're looking at is the frontier, and asking if we can find a way to live outside it without changing inputs.

As Molly shifts toward her specialty crop, she's able to trade more of her speciaty to Andrew, and Andrew is able to exchange for more of the good not his specialty. But the real magic happens when we move both Molly AND Andrew toward their specialty's, extending the range of possible trades.
