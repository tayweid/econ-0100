*This extends A3 by showing various not-fully specialized points.*

If the trade line lies below the PPF, the trade doesn't do better than the PPF for the farmer. So we'll only highlight the achievable area made possible by the trade. We also need to realize that not every trade is feasible, though, since the other farmer can only trade away what they're harvesting. So we constrain the trade line on its upper end as well. This leaves us with a line representing the PPF as well as a line representing the range of possible trades.

When thinking about trades, we have two levers. First, the exchange rate, represented by the slope of the trade line. If the trade is too steep, it's great for one farmer and terrible for the other. And if it's too flat, the same but reversed. So the only trades that are acceptable happen when the exchange rate lies between the opportunity costs of the two farmers.

And second, we can choose how much of each goods the farmers grow. Earlier we showed that Molly has a comparative advantage in spinach and Andrew has a comparative advantage in carrots so each is gonna specialize in each of these two goods. Each farmer specializing according to their comparative advantage, Molly harvests $40$ tons of spinach and Andrew harvests $8$ tons of carrots. This is like the co-op solution, but here we're looking for a trade that makes both farmers better off. And it's important to note here that we STILL haven't imposed preferences. All we're looking at is the frontier, and asking if we can find a way to live outside it without changing inputs.

As Molly shifts toward her specialty crop, she's able to trade more of her specialty to Andrew, and Andrew is able to exchange for more of the good not his specialty. But the real magic happens when we move both Molly AND Andrew toward their specialties, extending the range of possible trades.

At this point it becomes clarifying when we plot the trade lines on one graph for the farmers together.

Returning to the challenge we set out on, specializing, exchanging $3.5$ carrots for $10.5$ spinach sets the exchange rate to $3$, which works for both farmers, means both farmers have more of both goods than they had in autarky.
