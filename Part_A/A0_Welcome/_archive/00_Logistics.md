## Class 1 | Introductions

This is a great class. I took it my first year in undergrad. Saying it changed my life is maybe a bit hyperbolic. But the ideas are very deep in a way that really inspired me. If you’ve been on the fence, you made the right choice. In this room I’m going to challenge you and you’re going to leave with a much deeper understanding of the way the world works.

I took this class my first year of college. I was originally an engineering major, but in large part because of a big ahha moment we’ll talk through later in the semester, I added Economics as a major. Every semester when choosing classes to teach, I jockey for this one. I love this class and how it forms a foundational framework. One thing most people don’t tell you is that everyone in this building could take non-teaching jobs somewhere else. But teaching this material is so fun it’s worth it.

Before we go around and introduce ourselves, we’re going to do the first of many in-class simulations to demonstrate a core principle of economics.

Simulation:
Grading takes work and I like doing as little of it as possible. Who would be willing to skip every assignment in exchange for a guaranteed C? What about a guaranteed A?

If you’re struggling I need you to promise you’ll reach out to me. I aim to do the same, although you have to recognize I have a lot of students. So lets share the responsibility to make sure that you are staying current, getting what you need, and doing well.

And there are six Miniexams, which look a lot like Homework, just timed. They’re open book, open note, silent, and in airplane mode.

Classwork happens during the first five minutes of every class on Tuesday when there’s not a Miniexam. This is a small part of your grade, but you’ll get immediate feedback, and will help keep you on track.

All assignments are submitted via Box. You’ll need the Box app on Thursday. If this is a problem for you, let me know and we can find something that works. There’s a document scanner built into the app. You’ll complete the assignment inside the app and then scan any written work using the scanner. Both count. I care mostly about your process. If your process is off, the assignment will be off. When this happens, and it will happen for everyone here, I want to be able to check your process.

Signing this syllabus is a contract with yourself. You’re agreeing to assign yourself the reading, showing up to class, Classwork, 6 Homeworks, and 6 Miniexams.

Our time in class will be a mixture of Lectures, Vignettes, Simulations, and MiniExams.
