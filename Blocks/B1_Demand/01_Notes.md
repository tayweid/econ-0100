Last time we took a step toward answering the big questions left from Part A.

1. How do we coordinate many trades at the same time?
2. How do we pick an exchange rate when it changes who benefits from the exchanges taking place?
3. And how do we pick where to live on the PPF?

To answer these questions we need to be able to pick between different alternative points on the PPF. We need to think about what people want.

In your recitation you found preferences, quantity demanded, and the full collection of quantity demanded, the individual demand curve. You saw that your actual preferences look a little curved, but we’ll approximate this with a linear relationship to make things easy in this class. And you saw that as price goes down, people want to buy MORE.

Like you saw in the simulation, we can ask anyone for their preferences about anything. Amanda-Grace tries to stay healthy by eating foods like spinach and carrots but she also loves chocolate. To get her preferences for chocolate I can ask how many chocolate bars she would be willing to buy at \$4. That’s a pretty steep price for just a single bar, so Amanda-Grace tells me zero. Shes willing to buy zero chocolate bars at \$4. Amanda-Grace’s quantity demanded at \$4 is 0. We can plot this as a point on the price quantity graph.

#### Closer

Demand as a relationship between my willingness and ability to pay and my quantity demanded is pernicious and complex in some important ways. For example, what if I don’t have money? I’m willing but unable to pay. The demand of a rich person will be higher than the demand of a poor person, even if their subjective experience of a chocolate bar is in some sense the same. Keep these limitations in mind.

The demand curve lands us squarely in the ballpark of being able to resolve the question about how we choose the prices that coordinate how much sellers sell and how much buyers buy. Next time we’ll build the same set of ideas for the other side of the market: sellers. Then we’ll ask what it costs them to say yes to a trade.
