## 1. Decisions (Taylor, 2026-09-22)

Do not re-litigate these in the notes pass or the storyboard.

1. **Seven short scenes, not one long animation.** Each carries one claim and can be rendered, exported, and presented on its own.
2. **No one-at-a-time growth.** Stages are 1×1 → 2×1 → 2×2 → the full market. Bidding, switching, one price, and deliberation are taught on the small stages, then shown for one or two people inside the big one.
3. **Equilibrium is named at scene 2** on the smallest case, as "no one wants to switch," and re-earned at scene 6 where the counts match.
4. **The price window ($2–$6) appears only in scene 1.**
5. **One person per bar.** A buyer who wants more than one stands twice.
6. **The big market is the algebra market.** Demand P = 12 − Q/5, supply P = 2 + Q/20, Q in thousands of pounds. One person = one 1,000-lb lot at the per-pound price. Counts in the plaza equal the algebra's Qd and Qs at $3, $4, $5, $6 (see §4 for the quarter-dollar caveat).
7. **One market price line, stepped by Taylor, plus one boxed deliberation per direction.** Not decentralized per-seller price discovery in the big market.
8. **Sorted head-on rows build each curve;** the curve lifts to a side graph; the camera then pulls out to the plaza, where checks and circles happen.
9. **Build the whole thing now.** Scenes 4–6 first: they carry the mechanism and are the assets B4's price controls reuse.
10. **Class 09 (Wed Sep 23) does not depend on this.** Quick graph-centred review of the current export, straight to Exercise Q2, then B4 (§11).

## 3. Shared grammar (all scenes)

**Marks in the world.**
- **Check** above a person: willing at the current price (MB ≥ P for a buyer, MC ≤ P for a seller; a bar touching the line counts, B1's convention). Counts toward Qd or Qs.
- **Circle** on the ground: trading at this price. Circles go to the first people in the sorted row (highest MB, lowest MC) — see §4 rationing.
- A check without a circle is the shortage or the excess: willing, left out. These are the people who move the price.
- **Tag** beside a person: what they are offering now. Willing buyers show the price; unwilling buyers show their MB. Willing sellers show the price; unwilling sellers show their MC. In the crowd, tags appear only on the named people and the two or three nearest the line, never on all 59/100 (legibility).
- **Price line:** one red line, solid when it is the going price, dashed while proposed. Same meaning in the plaza and on every graph.
- Colour is a noun (style guide §2): checks in the side's colour (teal/orange), circles green. Taylor said "green check"; if he prefers green for both, that is the one exception to note.

**Counts.** Whenever a single going price is on screen, print the counts — on fingers in scenes 2–3 ("2 want to buy, 1 can sell"), on the graph's Q axis from scene 4 on: **Qd**, **Qs**, and **Qx** (exchanged = circles = the smaller of the two). Counts are generated from the §4 formulas, never typed, and must match the checks and circles in the world at every frame, including mid-play.

**Deliberation card** (scenes 2, 3, 6). Bottom caption band, two boxes side by side, words only:

```
Stay: Molly asks $6.25             Switch: outbid Gary at Andrew, $5.25
gain $7 − $6.25 = $0.75            gain $7 − $5.25 = $1.75
```

Affordable options get the side's check; unaffordable ones "✗ > MB $6" (or "< MC"). The chosen box gets the green circle. On advance the choice plays in the world. Each card has a question above it that the room can answer, and the room answers **before** the reveal.

**Stops and text.** Every stop has new on-screen text or a question. No navigation-only stops. Titles: azure flush-left questions. Captions are short (a line); anything longer below is narration for the notes, in Taylor's words (§12), not on-screen text. Cards: gold term, bottom band, arrow to the referent in the world; two lines allowed.

**Persistent record.** During the scene 6 plays, the prices tried stay visible as a faint ladder on the graph so the path is readable after the motion stops.

Qs equals 20(P − 2) at every quarter-dollar price. Qd equals 5(12 − P) at $3, $4, $5, $6 (multiples of $0.20) and is the whole-person count (rounded down) in between. At $3, $4, $6 one person per side sits exactly on the line; say once that the last one in is just indifferent (B4 uses this).

**Named people in the crowd:** Amanda-Grace = buyer 25 ($7), Gary = buyer 30 ($6), Andrew = seller 40 ($4). Molly's $2 is the supply intercept ("Molly is where the supply line starts"), not a crowd member.

**Rationing.** At a price where Qd ≠ Qs, circles go to the first Qx people in the sorted row (highest MB / lowest MC). This is what B4's surplus arithmetic under a price control assumes. At $3 that leaves Amanda-Grace (#25) and Gary (#30) out; at $6 it leaves Andrew (#40) unsold — the named people the deliberations use. The "line-jumping" story (who really gets served under a ceiling) is B4's, not B3's.

**Axes** (every crowd graph, scenes 4–7): Q 0–100 (ticks every 20), P 0–13 (ticks every 2), same x/y lengths as the current `4.c` axes (`03_Equilibrium.py:1295`), re-ranged from 0–90. Demand line (0, 12)→(60, 0); supply line (0, 2)→(100, 7). Buyer n's bar spans [n−1, n] with its top-right corner on the line (B1's convention); sellers likewise. Supply row = 100 sellers (MC up to $7.00), so a $7 world price (B6) has room.

**Tick size (open, §10):** $0.25 ticks match the small cast but land the demand read inside a bar at $3.25/$3.75/$4.25 (Qd prints the whole-person count: 43, 41, 38). $0.20 ticks would give whole-person counts on both sides at every tick.
