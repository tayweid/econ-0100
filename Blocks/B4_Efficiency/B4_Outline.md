# B4 Outline

Do welfare at the market level. 

1. Show one exchange, setting the price very low with a price control, thinking through quantity exchanged, and so on.
2. Then thinking about incentives in the price control, and then a move to equilibrium, and how welfare changed.
3. Then more quickly show a high price, doing it with TS maybe here.
4. Then force a few more people to exchange and see what happens to TS.
5. Then do first welfare theorem.

## Nuances

- Price controls motivate the question of what’s best.
- Start at equilibrium, since B3 just showed that setting.
- The back half should carry the decisions and styling of the front half.
- Comparing welfare changes under a price control to equilibrium may live better in B5.
- The interpretation is not that price controls are bad. Welfare analysis is a toolset for evaluating them.
- Surplus means value created. That’s why we say excess.
- The theorem needs competitive markets and no externalities. Part E and Part C.
