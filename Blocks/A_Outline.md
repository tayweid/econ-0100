## Part A | Better choices can benefit everyone

Set the table by drawing a line around the part of the playground the series lives in.

We all have preferences. Preferences are rankings. We can’t have everything. This combination of preferences in the face of scarcity requires us to make choices with tradeoffs. The cost of a tradeoff is the value of what you give up, what we call **Opportunity Cost**. This notion of cost has implications for our choices. The Production Possibility Frontier (PPF) constrains what’s possible and responds to changes.

The transition out of Feudal Europe, through Mercantilism, into the industrial revolution in the 1600s and 1700s presented society at the time with some big questions about whether we could potentially do better than simply fighting for one’s own stockpile of gold. While the ideas that we’ll talk about also showed up at other places and times on earth, the tradition of economics traces it’s roots back to this time.

The world shaping discovery at the time was that a certain set of choices could actually allow us to reach points outside the PPF. If we minimize our opportunity costs by specializing in what we’re relatively good at and then trading with one another for everything else, then we can do better than we could in autarky. This combination of specialization and trade bends out the PPF, making everyone better off at the same time. This is one of the biggest ideas to ever grace humanity. This series develops the big picture of how we implement this idea.
