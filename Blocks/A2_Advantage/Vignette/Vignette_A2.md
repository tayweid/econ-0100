## Vignette A2 | ECON 0100 | Fall 2026

Colin Creevey can bake $20$ cornish pasties ($P$) or $5$ cauldron cakes ($C$) in one day. Katie Bell also bakes cornish pasties and cauldron cakes at a neighboring bakery. She can bake $15$ pasties or $8$ cakes in one day.

### Q1 | Absolute Advantage

Set up a production table with both Colin and Katie's output per day. Who has the absolute advantage (AA) in pasties? In cakes?

AA in P: \_________\_

AA in C: \_________\_

---

### Q2 | Comparative Advantage

Set up an opportunity cost table with Colin and Katie's opportunity costs for each good. Who has the comparative advantage (CA) in pasties? In cakes?

CA in P: \_________\_

CA in C: \_________\_

---

### Q3 | Better at Both

Suppose Katie buys a new oven and can now bake $25$ pasties or $8$ cakes in one day. Update both tables. Who has the absolute advantage in each good now? Who has the comparative advantage in each good?

AA in P: \_________\_  AA in C: \_________\_

CA in P: \_________\_  CA in C: \_________\_

<!-- ANSWER KEY (not for students)
Production: Colin 20P or 5C; Katie 15P or 8C. OC: Colin 1C = 4P, 1P = 1/4C; Katie 1C = 15/8P, 1P = 8/15C.
Q1: AA in P Colin (20 > 15); AA in C Katie (8 > 5).
Q2: CA in P Colin (1/4 < 8/15); CA in C Katie (15/8 < 4).
Q3: Katie 25P or 8C, so OC 1C = 25/8P, 1P = 8/25C. AA in both goods Katie. CA in P still Colin (1/4 < 8/25); CA in C Katie (25/8 < 4). Better at both, but a comparative advantage in only one.
