## Vignette A1 | ECON 0100 | Fall 2026

Colin Creevey can bake $20$ cornish pasties ($P$) or $5$ cauldron cakes ($C$) in one day. Set up Colin's PPF on an $x,y$ graph with pasties ($P$) on the vertical and cakes ($C$) on the horizontal. Label the axes and the intercepts.

*(graph paper)*

### Q1 | Opportunity Cost

What is Colin's opportunity cost of producing $1$ cake? Of $1$ pasty? Which of the two does the slope of his PPF show?

Opportunity cost of 1C: \_________\_

Opportunity cost of 1P: \_________\_

The slope shows the opportunity cost of: \_________\_

---

### Q2 | Feasibility

Suppose Colin bakes $10$ pasties and $2$ cakes in one day. Is this inefficient, efficient, or unattainable? Use a graph or algebra to justify your answer.

Inefficient, Efficient, Unattainable: \_________\_

---

### Q3 | Next Best Alternative

Colin has one free afternoon. He could bake for the Gryffindor party, photograph the Quidditch match, or take a nap. He'd rather bake than do either of the others, and he'd rather photograph the match than nap. What is Colin's opportunity cost of baking? Then Dennis invites him to Hogsmeade, which Colin likes more than photographing the match but less than baking. What is his opportunity cost of baking now?

Opportunity cost of baking: \_________\_

After the invitation: \_________\_

<!-- ANSWER KEY (not for students)
PPF: P = 20 - 4C. Q1: OC of 1C = 4 pasties; OC of 1P = 1/4 cake; the slope (rise over run, pasties per cake) shows the OC of a cake, the horizontal-axis good.
Q2: at C = 2 the frontier allows 12 pasties, and 10 < 12, so inefficient.
Q3: photographing the match; then the Hogsmeade trip.
