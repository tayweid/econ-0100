#### Preferences + Scarcity = Choices

I like to boil this down into a psudomathematical relationship like this. When we like some things more than others but can't have everything, we have to pick the things we like the best, which involves giving up things that we like less.

***Preferences + Scarcity = Choices***

This is the world economics lives in. Given that we are making choices to maximize our preferences amidst scarcity, everything we do requires us to give up something. In some ways this is kinda dismal I'd say.



#### Tradeoffs

To get a better handle on choices, lets be a bit more sophisticated how we define tradeoffs. Lets say I give you the choice of $A$ OR $B$. 

***Show A or B***

If you choose $A$, you can't have $B$. If you choose $B$, you can't have $A$. If you like $A$ more than $B$, you'll pick $A$, which means you give up $B$. 

***Show choosing A and not choosing B***

This is what economists call opportunity cost, the value of the next best alternative. Here if you choose $A$, your next best alternative is $B$. So your opportunity cost of $A$ is $B$. And if you like $B$ more than $A$, you would pick $B$ and the opportunity cost of $B$ is $A$. We’re going to use opportunity cost quite heavily as we build more sophisticated models.
